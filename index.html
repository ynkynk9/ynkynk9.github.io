Selected project / 01

Interactive Data Visualization

Helping learners turn complex information into understandable, interactive visual stories.

Overview

A project-based learning approach connecting data literacy, visual communication and front-end development.

Background

Data visualization was taught alongside web design and programming in higher education settings.

Challenge

Students needed to move beyond making charts and learn how to communicate a meaningful insight to a real audience.

My Role

Course designer, lecturer and project mentor.

Process

Frame a question, explore and clean data, select visual encodings, build a web prototype, and refine through critique.

Outcome

A reusable learning framework that connected analysis, communication and making in one continuous workflow.

Reflection

The strongest learning happened when students treated visualization as a product and communication problem, not only a technical exercise.

Selected project / 02

WebVR Campus Experience

An exploration of web-based immersive media for campus culture and orientation.

Overview

A 3D panoramic web experience concept for making campus stories more discoverable.

Background

Campus culture is commonly communicated through static pages and linear tours.

Challenge

Create a sense of place without sacrificing clarity, accessibility or browser-based reach.

My Role

Researcher and interaction designer.

Process

Defined orientation principles, mapped interaction cues and developed a layered content approach for panoramic exploration.

Outcome

The work informed a 2022 publication on web-based interactive VR and 3D panoramic campus design.

Reflection

Immersive technology is most useful when it supports a visitor’s understanding rather than competing for attention.

Selected project / 03

AI for Sustainable Decision Making

An independent research proposal about human-centered AI and more understandable sustainable choices.

Overview

A proposal for exploring how AI-supported interfaces could help people evaluate sustainability-related decisions.

Background

Sustainable choices often involve fragmented data, uncertainty and difficult trade-offs.

Challenge

Consider how assistance can make complexity clearer without presenting automated recommendations as unquestionable answers.

My Role

Independent researcher and proposal author.

Process

Outlined user questions, information needs, interaction principles and ethical considerations for a future research direction.

Outcome

A focused concept that connects product thinking, behavior change and responsible AI design.

Reflection

The value of AI in this context should be measured by better human understanding and agency, not automation alone.
